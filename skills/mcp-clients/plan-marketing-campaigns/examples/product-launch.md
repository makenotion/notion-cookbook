# Turn a product launch brief into campaigns and working drafts

This fictional example shows a small marketing team preparing a launch. It
illustrates the connected pages and their contents; adapt the structure to the
team's actual work.

## Starting brief

> Plan the launch of Fieldnote, our interview-notes app for independent researchers.
> The message is “Finish the interview. Keep the insight.” Researchers can tag
> notes and collect selected quotes in a research summary. We do not have automatic
> transcription. Target launch is November 18, 2026, pending product review.
>
> Maya leads the campaign and is writing an announcement email for November 18.
> Leo is preparing a 30-second walkthrough, also planned for November 18; no footage
> exists yet. Please draft both from this brief. We also have an undated idea for a
> post about organizing quotes, with no owner yet.
>
> Separately, our December research roundtable is in planning. Maya has an invitation
> outline, but no event date or venue. Keep that separate from the product launch.
>
> Use a calm, practical voice: specific language, no “effortless,” no invented
> customer results. Our colors are ink blue and pale mint. Create simple original
> title artwork for the two campaigns and a small cover. Nothing is approved for
> publication yet, and the signup URL is still being confirmed.

## Marketing home: Fieldnote · Marketing

Use full width and the default font. Give the home a blue megaphone icon and a
shallow ink-blue cover with a mint line motif. Original campaign artwork carries
only the campaign title and the same motif; store it as proposed creative inside
the campaign record. It is not a product screenshot or approved brand asset.

The first screen has a narrow left column and a wider right column:

| Left: navigation and next action                                      | Right: active work                                                                       |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 📣 Campaigns                                                          | **Active campaigns**, a pale-mint heading above two small gallery cards                  |
| ✍️ Content                                                            | **Finish the interview. Keep the insight.** · In production · Nov 18, 2026 · Maya        |
| 🎨 Brand resources                                                    | **Research roundtable** · Planning · Maya; no invented launch date                       |
| **Next: review the announcement** — links directly to the email draft | **Needs review**, a compact list with the announcement email and “Product review needed” |

The gallery uses the proposed campaign title artwork as previews. Keep the
cover shallow enough that the review list remains visible on an ordinary desktop
screen. Do not repeat a welcome paragraph, campaign slogan, or database title
above the cards. The next-action link opens the draft rather than a general index.

Campaign icons are a blue megaphone and green conversation bubbles. Content pages
use a blue envelope, purple video camera, green note, and green invitation icon.
Colored icons carry through to page mentions and relations.

## Connected campaigns and content

Campaigns and Content are child databases of the marketing home. Brand resources
is a separate child page. Campaign cards open database records, and every content
record has a native Campaign relation. The campaign's linked Content view filters
on that exact relation; it does not copy records.

| Campaign                                | Stage         | Target launch | Lead |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------- | ------------- | ---- |
| Finish the interview. Keep the insight. | In production | Nov 18, 2026  | Maya |
| Research roundtable                     | Planning      | Empty         | Maya |

The launch date remains a target pending product review. Resolve Maya and Leo to
actual people only when the connected team provides a matching identity; their
names in the brief are not sufficient to guess an account.

| Content                                    | Campaign            | Format | Production    | Approval      | Planned publish date | Owner |
| ------------------------------------------ | ------------------- | ------ | ------------- | ------------- | -------------------- | ----- |
| Meet Fieldnote                             | Product launch      | Email  | In review     | Pending       | Nov 18, 2026         | Maya  |
| From interview notes to a research summary | Product launch      | Video  | In production | Not requested | Nov 18, 2026         | Leo   |
| Three ways to group interview quotes       | Product launch      | Post   | Idea          | Not requested | Empty                | Empty |
| Join the research roundtable               | Research roundtable | Email  | Outline       | Not requested | Empty                | Maya  |

“Product launch” in this table means the relation to **Finish the interview. Keep
the insight.**, not a second campaign record. The newly generated email is in
review because the example hands it to Maya for review; it remains unapproved.
All four items are unpublished. Planned publication dates are tentative, not
scheduled sends.

Content opens on a production board grouped by production state. Cards show
format, owner when known, and planned publish date; omit a duplicate status chip.
The **Publishing calendar** shows the two dated launch items. **No publish date**
shows the quote post and roundtable invitation. A **Needs review** list shows the
announcement email with its pending approval. Each record exposes Campaign,
Production, Approval, Owner, and Planned publish date near the title; supporting
creative and longer notes stay in the body.

## Campaign page: Finish the interview. Keep the insight

Pin Stage, Target launch, and Lead near the title. Below them, show this brief:

**Promise:** Keep selected interview quotes connected to a usable research summary.

**Audience:** Independent researchers who already take interview notes and need
to organize what they learned.

**Action:** Explore Fieldnote when the signup destination is confirmed.

**Decision needed:** Product review must confirm the description of tagging and
summary creation before the November 18 target can become a launch commitment.

Follow the brief with **Launch content**, a filtered list containing exactly the
three launch items, including the undated quote-post idea. Show Format, Production,
and Planned publish date. The roundtable invitation appears only on its own
campaign page and in shared Content views.

Keep proposed title artwork under **Campaign creative**, after the working list.
A short link to Brand resources gives writers access to voice and product facts.

## Email page: Meet Fieldnote

**Draft · pending product review**

**Subject:** Finish the interview. Keep the insight.

**Preview text:** Turn selected interview quotes into a research summary.

**Body:**

You have finished the interview. Now you need to make sense of the notes.

Fieldnote helps independent researchers tag their notes and collect selected
quotes in a research summary. Group what belongs together, then keep the words
that support your findings close to the summary you are writing.

If your next step is sorting through interview notes, take a look at Fieldnote.

**Call to action:** Explore Fieldnote — signup URL to be confirmed.

**Before approval:**

- Confirm the description of tagging and collecting quotes with product.
- Add the confirmed signup URL and check the destination.
- Confirm the November 18 publication plan. This draft is not scheduled or sent.

The Campaign property returns to the brief. A short Content link returns to the
shared production board. No transcription claim, time-saving statistic, customer
quote, or invented product screenshot appears in the draft.

## Video page: From interview notes to a research summary

**Working storyboard · footage needed**

| Time   | Proposed picture                                           | Draft narration                                                |
| ------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0–6s   | Title card, then an interview-notes example                | “The interview is done. What do you want to keep?”             |
| 6–16s  | Planned capture of tagging notes                           | “Group notes with tags so related observations stay together.” |
| 16–25s | Planned capture of collecting selected quotes in a summary | “Bring the quotes you need into your research summary.”        |
| 25–30s | End card using the campaign title treatment                | “Finish the interview. Keep the insight. Explore Fieldnote.”   |

**Leo's next step:** Capture the two product sequences after product confirms the
workflow. Use fictional interview text clearly labeled as sample content.

**Review before recording:** Confirm the sequence matches the actual product,
check that no private research appears on screen, and obtain the signup destination
for the end card. The storyboard is ready to discuss; footage is not ready.

## Brand resources

Use an artist-palette icon and short mint-highlighted headings. This is a readable
guide, not an empty asset gallery.

**Voice**

Practical, calm, and specific. Address the researcher directly. Describe the next
step without promising an outcome the product has not demonstrated.

| Use                                              | Avoid                                    |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------- |
| “Collect selected quotes in a research summary.” | “Instant insights from every interview.” |
| “Group notes with tags.”                         | “Effortlessly automate your research.”   |

**Product facts**

- Supports tagging notes and collecting selected quotes in a research summary.
- Does not provide automatic transcription.
- Signup destination and launch commitment await confirmation.

**Visual direction**

Ink blue for identity and icons; pale mint for section headings. Reuse the same
line motif across the home cover and campaign title cards. Keep proposed artwork
with its campaign so it cannot be mistaken for an approved asset library.

## First useful action

The user opens the home, sees the upcoming launch and its target date, and opens
**Meet Fieldnote** from **Next: review the announcement**. The draft and its three
review questions are ready. The campaign relation leads back to the brief and its
three deliverables; shared Content also contains the separate roundtable invitation.
Unknown dates, ownership, footage, and approval remain explicit without blocking
this first review.
