# Design an editorial workspace

Begin with the thing being published and the production rhythm. A publication
calendar, campaign launch, and asset library are different editorial products.

## Research to use

The inspected preview of [Brand Guide by Matt Bio](https://www.notion.com/templates/brand-guide-bio)
uses a high-contrast typographic cover, compact index, and coordinated brand
panels. Borrow its editorial confidence and coherent identity.
[Brand Assets by Notion](https://www.notion.com/templates/brand-assets) shows large
asset previews, short labels, and category tabs: useful when people recognize
creative by sight. The [content calendar reference](template.md) supplies the
production model. Previews were inspected on October 29, 2026; they do not prove
underlying automation or interactions.

## Let production lead

For a dated launch or publication, bring the publishing calendar near the top,
with production and unscheduled work as alternate views on the same collection.
For a team still making assets, make production the primary view instead. Choose
from the actual content state. Do not hide undated ideas in a calendar or claim
an unapproved item is scheduled for publication.

Use a compact campaign masthead with the actual message and audience. Give the
work surface room; do not push it below a welcome essay and paired callouts.
An editorial brief can sit below the schedule or in a side rail. Approval cues
belong on the content items so the user can act on them.

Let creative lead visually when real assets exist: image previews, consistent
cropping, short labels. With text-only work, use strong editorial hierarchy and
concise channel labels. Do not fabricate product screenshots, use unrelated
stock images, or disguise an empty gallery as finished creative.

Record pages should feel like briefs: intended audience, angle, draft or outline,
review questions, and delivery requirements. Separate proposed copy from approved
copy. Use the brand's voice here; do not impose the study planner's warmth or a
technical wiki's tone on every marketing team.

## Judge the result

Can an editor identify the next publication, find what needs review, and open a
usable brief? Verify calendar dates, approval states, and undated visibility.
Inspect cards and the calendar at the available width; shorten visible metadata
before squeezing everything into narrow cards. Check actual rendering when
available and state when it was not inspected.

Each campaign demo has an independent private home. A comparison index can link
to it. Choose page width, imagery, and density for production when supported;
these settings are not supplied by a parent page or by changing an emoji.
