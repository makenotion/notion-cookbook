# Adapt the plan to the work

Read the section matching the request. Use its questions to identify consequential
missing context, not as a mandatory questionnaire. Prefer known information and
build a useful initial plan before asking for optional refinements.

## Events

Useful context: date, intended audience or guest count, budget, hosts, and what is
already confirmed. Order long-lead decisions before invitations and final counts:
venue, food, speakers or activities, then materials and the day-of run sheet.
Keep booking deadlines separate from the event date. Record estimated and
confirmed costs distinctly and retain their currency.

Use Tasks for preparations and a separate Guests collection when tracking
attendance is part of the request. Guest fields can include Name, Contact, RSVP,
and Plus-ones. Do not mark someone Invited merely because they are on a draft
list. Draft invitations in a working page, with date, venue, response deadline,
and unresolved details clearly distinguished. Collect responses through a form
only when requested or appropriate to the authorized workflow; verify its audience
and actual connection to the guest records before describing it as ready.

## Trips

Start from the chosen destination, dates, travelers, budget, and confirmed bookings.
Researching which destination to choose is a separate task. Organize transport and
lodging before scarce activities. Use itinerary records for scheduled activities
and bookings, with dates, local times and timezones where relevant, place, source,
and confirmation state. Keep preparation tasks separate from timed itinerary items.

Preserve confirmation links and cancellation details from the source. Sum known
costs only, state what is excluded, and do not combine currencies without a stated
conversion. An estimated amount is not a paid expense. Include documents, packing,
and bookings to confirm as a useful checklist. Verify current information through
appropriate sources before recommending availability, prices, or requirements.

## Job searches

Use target roles, location or remote preference, and the user's constraints to
shape the plan. Do not impose an application quota; propose a cadence only when
helpful and label it as proposed. Keep the base résumé and a story bank grounded
in actual achievements, with tailored materials attached to their application.

Use one application record per role, with Company, Role, Source, Stage, Date
applied, Next action, Follow-up date, and Contact where known. Interested, Applied,
Interviewing, Offer, and Closed are distinct states. Do not advance an application
because a draft was created. Show follow-ups and interviews prominently; retain
undated opportunities. Interview pages contain relevant company context, questions,
and evidence-based stories, not invented accomplishments.

## Launches

Identify what goes live, for whom, and the target or committed date. Include only
relevant workstreams, such as product, marketing, support, sales, or measurement.
Work backward to a readiness decision with time to resolve blockers. Connect
engineering implementation to its existing plan rather than recreating its tasks.

Use one record per deliverable, with workstream, owner, review state, and due date
where known. Draft the requested announcement, help article, or launch checklist
inside its record. Identify the conditions for proceeding and what to defer if
something slips. Keep a results section for actual post-launch evidence; targets
are not results. Link sites and creative assets to the relevant deliverable and
retain the user's preferred tools.

## Marketing campaigns

Use audience, message, offer, channels, and launch date to establish a brief.
Work backward through draft, review, final asset, and publication steps; propose
buffer appropriate to the available time rather than assuming a fixed week.
Use campaign records related to their content when several campaigns or an
ongoing program are involved. Keep scope small for a single campaign.

Each content record contains the requested copy or script, channel, format,
production state, planned publication date, and review questions. Distinguish
production from approval and actual publication. Keep concept artwork, finished
assets, and product screenshots distinguishable. Use existing brand guidance and
the user's design tools; do not add a vendor or an external site just to complete
a template. Store resulting assets with their content records.

## Recurring content

Use channels, a sustainable posting rhythm, themes, and voice to shape a calendar.
For “I need to post more,” propose a manageable rhythm rather than inventing an
agreed commitment. One record per post holds its theme, channel, draft, visual
idea, and planned date. Include an undated view alongside the calendar.

On later runs, inspect existing posts and extend the same calendar for the
requested horizon. Preserve published items and approvals. Show upcoming gaps and
review work, then write the requested content. A visual idea does not imply that
a finished asset exists. Do not create RSVP forms or meeting pages unless the
request needs them.

## Moves and other plans

Define what done looks like, the deadline if known, and who is involved. Sequence
checkable milestones and identify dependencies: for a move, access to the new
home precedes delivery; a confirmed date precedes booking movers. Prepare useful
packing, handoff, or purchasing lists from the brief. Keep quotes, estimates,
reservations, and paid expenses distinct. A short plan can remain a checklist;
a larger one benefits from owned, dated tasks and connected detail pages.
