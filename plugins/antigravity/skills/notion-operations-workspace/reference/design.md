# Design a service directory

The home should answer “how do I get this done?” for someone who does not know
which team or database owns the process. Navigation and dependable instructions
matter more than a dashboard of activity.

## Research to use

The inspected [Team home by Notion](https://www.notion.com/templates/team-home-v4)
preview uses a home identity, team overview, and concise mission to orient the
reader. [Brand Guidelines by Notion](https://www.notion.com/templates/brand-guidelines)
uses headings and dividers to make reference material legible. Borrow those
orientation and reading patterns, together with the [structural reference](template.md).
These are October 29, 2026 preview observations, not an audit of live features.

## Organize around requests

Make the directory the dominant surface. Use plain-language entry points such
as “Set up a laptop” and “Buy software,” with a sentence explaining when to choose
each. Group a larger handbook by reader intent, not the internal database schema.
A compact index or numbered chapters can work better than colored cards.

Give procedures a consistent, serious reading treatment: prerequisites, ordered
steps, approval checkpoints, and completion checks. Distinguish draft guidance
from approved policy near the text that could be acted on. Do not invent policy
or approval authority to make a handbook seem complete.

Put request tracking in a secondary desk or compact section so frequent operators
can reach it without making every employee read the queue first. Show status and
missing ownership where work is handled. The home does not need a board, a hero
slogan, and a callout for every route.

Onboarding should be a usable route through real procedures. Avoid duplicating
those procedures as a separate checklist unless it tracks a distinct instance.
Contact and escalation details belong near the process only when known.

## Judge the result

Test a first-time employee finding a procedure and an operator finding an open
request. Each route must end in useful content and return to the directory.
Check instructions, ownership uncertainty, and approval wording. Inspect the
reading order and density at available widths; report if rendering is unavailable.
A polished directory is allowed to contain little color and few databases.

Give each demo its own private home. Use an index only for cross-demo discovery;
do not compose the handbook as a chapter in a generic demo dashboard. Page width
and typography, when supported, should serve readable procedures.
